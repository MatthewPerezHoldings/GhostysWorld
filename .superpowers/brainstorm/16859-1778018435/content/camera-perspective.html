How does the camera see Ghosty's World?

This is the single biggest design choice — it shapes everything: art, controls, level design, and how the dogs hunt the mailman.

A

Top-down (bird's-eye)

Think: classic Zelda, Pokémon, Hotline Miami.

Camera looks straight down at the yard. Mailman walks in from the gate, navigates around obstacles (bushes, garden gnomes, kiddie pool). Ghost and the Golden each patrol zones — you sneak/dash to the door slot.

Top-down — fence around the yard, door at top, mailman at bottom

Best for: stealth, level layout creativity, multiple dogs visible at once, future levels (different houses).

B

Side-scroller

Think: Mario, Crash Bandicoot, old Sonic.

Camera from the side. Mailman runs left → right toward the front door. Jumps over Ghost, slides under the Golden, dodges thrown bones. Reaches the door, slot opens, drops mail in.

Side view — sky above, ground below, mailman runs right

Best for: tight, snappy gameplay; easy to grasp; great for short replayable runs ("got farther this time!").

C

Single-screen "puzzle arena"

Think: Pac-Man, Bomberman, old arcade single-board games.

Whole front yard fits on one screen — no scrolling. Mailman starts at the gate, must reach the door slot. Ghost and the Golden have AI patrol patterns you have to read. Each level is a new yard layout.

Whole yard visible at once — pure cat-and-mouse

Best for: "one more try" puzzle vibe; each level designed by hand; clearest showcase for Ghost-the-dog as a character.

My pick: C — single-screen puzzle arena. It makes Ghost the star (you can see him patrolling the whole time), it's the easiest to make great quickly, and "deliver the mail without getting caught" maps perfectly onto an arcade-puzzle loop. But if you want a longer-running, side-scrolly experience, B is the move.
